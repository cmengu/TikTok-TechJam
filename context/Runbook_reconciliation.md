## Precedence rule — when the documents disagree

1. The starter kit (`README.md`, `evaluate.py`, `submit.py`, `data.py`) — it defines the score and the submission format, and it is not ours to change.
2. `Pivot_sequence` — it was written after reading the kit and encodes the kit's contract (temporal splits, `row_id`, delegate to `evaluate()`, numpy-only, `submit.py --check` as the gate).
3. `Execution_runbook` — the *mechanism* is right and more detailed than the Pivot (audit findings A–I, the two `_build_env` traps, the type chain, anti-short-circuit doctrine), but Steps 2, 3 and 6 were written against a remembered Ali-CCP-shaped data contract, and their code blocks contradict rule 1. Take the runbook's *sequence and tests*; take the kit's *data contract*.

The one place the runbook beats the Pivot is the merge order (§C1 below) — verified in git, not on paper.
